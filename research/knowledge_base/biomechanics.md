# Biomechanics and anthropometric adaptation

**Runtime status:** conditional, normative when loaded

**Load when:** equipment fit, usable ROM, segment proportions, instability,
discomfort, repeated technique difficulty, or poor response makes exercise
geometry relevant

**Do not load merely because:** height, weight, BMI, or a body-type label exists.

The central rule is:

> Anthropometry proposes an experiment; controlled performance, tolerance, and
> longitudinal response decide whether the variation fits.

Biomechanical changes redistribute demand. They do not make an exercise
globally safe, eliminate joint moments, or establish a universally correct
technique.

## Operational rules

| ID | Rule | Confidence | Conditions | Exceptions / limits | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `BM-001` | Use height primarily for rack, bench, machine, handle, and start-position fit. | Moderate | Equipment dimensions or setup may limit usable ROM or stability | Do not map a height threshold directly to an exercise or training dose. | EBD, “Tall versus short” |
| `BM-002` | Use femur, tibia, torso, and usable ankle/hip ROM to propose squat variants to test: stance adjustment, heel elevation, front/goblet loading, box-limited ROM, hack squat, or leg press. | Low-moderate for prediction; Moderate for candidate-trial approach | Squat setup, balance, depth, or symptoms indicate a geometry issue | No segment-ratio threshold makes one variation mandatory; wider stance can shift demand toward the hip. | EBD, “Squat mechanics”; DOI `10.1519/JSC.0000000000004949` |
| `BM-003` | Treat conventional, sumo, trap-bar, Romanian, elevated, cable, and machine hinges as candidates for a hip-extension objective; compare controlled setup, tolerance, progression, and response. | Moderate for non-determinism; Low-moderate for pre-trial ranking | A hinge is appropriate and equipment is available | Do not assign style solely from arm length, torso length, or femur length. | EBD, “Deadlift mechanics”; Cholewa et al. 2019 |
| `BM-004` | For pressing, test moderate grip-width, implement, angle, support, and ROM changes when shoulder/elbow response or arm length makes them relevant. | Low-moderate for individual prediction | Press setup or symptoms justify a variation trial | Narrower and wider grips redistribute shoulder and elbow demand; neither is universally safer. Dumbbells do not guarantee a particular shoulder rotation. | EBD, “Bench-press and pressing mechanics”; Mausehund et al. 2022 |
| `BM-005` | Use body mass and soft-tissue constraints to improve accessibility through support, seating, stance/ROM options, machine fit, or lower-impact cardio. | Moderate | The user reports an actual accessibility, balance, comfort, or tolerance issue | BMI does not define sets, reps, RIR, frequency, exercise quality, or mandatory machine use. | EBD, “Overweight and obesity as an anthropometric variable” |
| `BM-006` | Use active, task-specific ROM and observed control to set exercise ROM. | Moderate; implementation rule | ROM affects setup, objective, or symptoms | Passive ROM alone does not determine functional capability; do not impose universal joint-angle windows. | EBD, “Anthropometric decision matrix” |
| `BM-007` | Treat machines and free weights as different constraint and resistance environments; choose by goal, fit, stability, preference, and response. | High for both being effective; Moderate for individual ranking | Both modalities are available | Machines do not eliminate lumbar moments, joint forces, stabilization, or injury risk; free weights are not inherently superior. | EBD scope conclusions; ACSM 2026 |
| `BM-008` | When changing stance, grip, load position, support, or ROM, update which region may receive more or less demand and monitor the result. | Moderate | Any biomechanical modification | Never describe a local reduction as removal of all stress or as proof of greater global safety. | EBD squat and pressing sections |
| `BM-009` | Keep biomechanical fit a moderate candidate-ranking signal; allow stable exercise-specific feedback to override it. | Moderate | Candidate scoring | Clinical red flags and clinician restrictions remain hard constraints and cannot be overridden by good performance. | EBD, “Proposed exercise-selection scoring” |

## Trial protocol

Change one major setup variable at a time when practical and record:

```text
exercise variation
stance / grip / implement / support
load and repetition range
usable ROM
RIR
technical confidence and stability
symptom location and behavior during the session
next-day or next-session response
```

Prefer the acceptable variation that the user can control, tolerate, progress,
recover from, and adhere to. A single comfortable session is weak evidence;
repeat exposure before treating the preference as stable when safety allows.

## Explicitly rejected deterministic rules

The following claims from the archived original biomechanics report are not
operational rules:

| Rejected claim | Controlled replacement |
| --- | --- |
| `more than 20 weekly sets = MRV` | Volume has diminishing returns; use individual recovery and progress rather than a universal ceiling. |
| `long femurs require heel elevation or machines` | Offer several squat variants to test; no validated limb-ratio cutoff exists. |
| `long arms require conventional deadlift; short arms require sumo` | Anthropometric associations are modest and insufficient for mandatory style selection. |
| `machines entirely negate lumbar moments or joint shear` | Machines change constraints and stability demands but do not eliminate internal loading. |
| `obesity requires machines and makes free-weight closed-chain work unacceptable` | Offer accessible supported options when they solve a reported constraint; body mass alone does not ban a modality. |
| `ectomorph or underweight status implies lower MRV` | Do not use somatotypes; determine dose from experience, health context, recovery, adherence, and response. |
| `dynamic spinal flexion is universally contraindicated` | Use controlled spinal motion when appropriate and tolerated; no universal ban follows from the reviewed evidence. |
| `a diagnosis or pain location mandates one exact ROM window` | Honor clinician restrictions and use symptom trajectory; do not generalize one biomechanical model into a universal prescription. |
| `machines perfectly match human strength curves` | Resistance profiles vary by machine and user geometry; verify fit and response. |
